\begin{abstract}
Drop the Handkerchief (DTH) is a fully observed, two-player, zero-sum
stochastic game with simultaneous exact-second actions, a hard 300-second
capacity, and a probabilistic revival rule. We solve it. The state space is
first collapsed by a per-player quotient: a player who can no longer survive
an injection has a revival probability of identically zero, so their
time-to-death coordinate is never read again and folds into a per-load
sentinel, reducing the $8.15\times10^{9}$-state envelope to
$289{,}374{,}121$ equivalence classes, a factor of $28.2$. A potential
function that strictly increases along every live transition organizes those
classes into $1{,}201$ layers and licenses a single backward sweep with no
search, no frontier, and no reachability analysis. Every class value is
accepted only with an explicit equilibrium certificate: a mixed-strategy pair
whose saddle gap against the fully reconstructed $60\times60$ stage matrix is
at most $10^{-6}$. The certificates come from a ladder of increasingly
expensive certified solvers --- an $O(60)$ pure-saddle test, a pinned
equalizer solve on guessed and on full supports, and linear programming ---
whose routing turned out to be dominated ($99.8\%$) by full-support
equilibria. The sweep was executed twice, by a Python reference
implementation and by a parallel Rust kernel constrained to bit-identical
arithmetic, and the two agree byte for byte on every value they both
computed. The value of the complete game at the initial state is
\[
V(0,0,0,0)=0.0898500728095116,
\]
so under optimal play the first Dropper wins with probability
$\approx0.5449$. The entire computation took about $1.7$ hours of wall clock
on one twelve-core consumer desktop, against a previously recorded
single-core projection of $3.7$--$5.1$ years.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

DTH is the pure timing core of the ``Surpassing the Leader'' wager from the
manga \emph{Usogui}; the rules modeled here are exactly those licensed by the
project's evidence ledger, and the game sits at rung L1 of the repository's
formulation ladder (\texttt{docs/FORMULATION\_LADDER.md}): literal
one-second actions, no leap-second window, no private information. Two
players alternate the roles of \emph{Dropper} and \emph{Checker}. Each round
both simultaneously commit to a second in $\{1,\dots,60\}$; the check
succeeds if it does not precede the drop, in which case the checker absorbs
the inclusive elapsed interval into a personal 300-second cylinder, and the
roles swap. A failed check injects the checker with their accumulated load
plus a fixed 60-second penalty; the injection is fatal unless a frozen,
monotone revival law saves them, in which case their cylinder resets and
their cumulative time dead grows. Overflowing the cylinder is fatal outright.
The last player alive wins.

The game is finite but large, stochastic, and genuinely simultaneous: its
stage games require mixed strategies, and its state space was, until now,
handled by bounded search around certified anchors. This paper records the
complete solution and the small amount of mathematics that made it cheap:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item a \emph{per-player quotient} (Section~\ref{sec:quotient}): death
  eligibility is absorbing and zeroes the only expression that ever reads a
  player's time-to-death, so that coordinate collapses to a sentinel
  independently for each player;
\item a \emph{potential} (Section~\ref{sec:potential}) that strictly
  increases along every live transition, so a single descending sweep sees
  every child before its parents --- eliminating search, reachability
  analysis, and frontier bookkeeping in one stroke;
\item a \emph{certificate discipline} (Section~\ref{sec:certified}): no
  value enters the table without a mixed-strategy pair whose one-step
  exploitability against the fully reconstructed stage matrix is at most
  $\tol=10^{-6}$, no matter which solver produced it --- so the internals of
  the fast solvers are irrelevant to soundness; and
\item the $61$-class \emph{Toeplitz structure} of the stage matrices
  (Section~\ref{sec:stage}), which makes certificates and pure-saddle tests
  $O(60)$ or $O(60k)$ instead of $O(60^2)$.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:computation} reports the computation itself, its
cross-implementation audit, and the error analysis; Section~\ref{sec:claims}
states precisely what is and is not established.

\section{The game}\label{sec:game}

\begin{definition}[State]\label{def:state}
A live state is $x=(s_c,t_c,s_d,t_d)\in\states$, written from the
perspective of the current \emph{Dropper}: $s_c\in\{0,\dots,299\}$ and
$t_c\in\{0,\dots,300\}$ are the current \emph{Checker's} accumulated load
(``squandered time'', ST) and cumulative time dead (TTD), and
$(s_d,t_d)$ are the Dropper's. Terminal outcomes are $\W$ (the mover wins,
utility $+1$) and its negation under the role swap.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Actions and timing]\label{def:actions}
Both players simultaneously choose seconds $d,c\in\{1,\dots,60\}$ (drop and
check). The check \emph{succeeds} iff $d\le c$, and then the checker's load
grows by the inclusive interval $\ell=c-d+1\in\{1,\dots,60\}$; equal seconds
cost one second, not zero. If $s_c+\ell\ge300$ the cylinder overflows and
the checker dies: the transition is terminal $\W$ for the Dropper.
Otherwise play continues from
\[
(s_c,t_c,s_d,t_d)\ \xrightarrow{\ \text{success, lag }\ell\ }\
(s_d,\,t_d,\,s_c+\ell,\,t_c),
\]
with the roles swapped (the old Checker drops next).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Failure, dose, and revival]\label{def:failure}
On a failed check ($d>c$) the checker is injected with the dose
$q=s_c+60$. Survival is possible iff
\begin{equation}\label{eq:survive}
q<300\ \wedge\ q+t_c\le300
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
s_c\le239\ \wedge\ s_c+t_c\le240,
\end{equation}
where the second inequality is deliberately non-strict: a resulting
cumulative TTD of exactly $300$ remains eligible. When
\eqref{eq:survive} holds, the checker revives with the frozen probability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:revival}
p(s_c,t_c)\;=\;0.95\left(1-\frac{s_c}{240}\right)0.75^{\,t_c/60},
\end{equation}
their cylinder resets, and their TTD grows by the dose:
\[
(s_c,t_c,s_d,t_d)\ \xrightarrow{\ \text{failure, revive}\ }\
(s_d,\,t_d,\,0,\,t_c+s_c+60)
\quad\text{with probability }p(s_c,t_c),
\]
else the transition is terminal $\W$. When \eqref{eq:survive} fails, the
injection is fatal with certainty and $p\equiv0$.
\end{definition}

Values are always from the current Dropper's perspective and negate across
the role swap; the game is zero-sum with terminal-only payoff in
$\{-1,+1\}$, so all values lie in $[-1,1]$. We call the checker profile
$(s,t)$ \emph{dead} when \eqref{eq:survive} fails --- the player is still
playing, but any future failed check is certainly fatal for them.

\section{Finiteness and stage structure}\label{sec:stage}

\begin{lemma}[Damage strictly increases]\label{lem:damage}
Let $R(x)=s_c+t_c+s_d+t_d$. Every live transition strictly increases $R$:
a successful lag $\ell$ adds $\ell\ge1$, and a revived failure adds exactly
$60$ (the dose $s_c+60$ enters TTD while the load $s_c$ resets).
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[The game is finite]\label{cor:finite}
$R\le299+300+299+300=1198$ on live states, so every play reaches a terminal
outcome within $1198$ live transitions. The game therefore has a unique
value $V:\states\to[-1,1]$, obtained by backward induction over the finite
acyclic transition graph~\cite{zermelo1913}, solving at each state the
zero-sum simultaneous stage game by von Neumann's minimax
theorem~\cite{vonneumann1928}; the construction is the finite, absorbing
case of a Shapley stochastic game~\cite{shapley1953}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[The 61 transition classes]\label{prop:classes}
Fix a live state $x$ and let $V$ assign values to its children. Define
\[
S_\ell=
\begin{cases}
+1,& s_c+\ell\ge300,\\
-V(s_d,t_d,s_c+\ell,t_c),&\text{else},
\end{cases}
\qquad
F=
\begin{cases}
+1,& (s_c,t_c)\ \text{dead},\\
p\,\bigl(-V(s_d,t_d,0,t_c+s_c+60)\bigr)+(1-p),&\text{else}.
\end{cases}
\]
Then the $60\times60$ stage matrix of $x$, with the Dropper as row player,
is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:toeplitz}
M(x)_{d,c}=
\begin{cases}
S_{c-d+1}, & c\ge d,\\
F, & c<d,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and $V(x)=\val(M(x))$. In particular $M(x)$ carries only $61$ distinct
entries: it is Toeplitz on and above the diagonal and constant below it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A successful cell depends on the actions only through the lag
$\ell=c-d+1$, and every failed cell has the identical transition
distribution; the entries are the expected continuation values, negated for
the role swap. The Bellman equation is Corollary~\ref{cor:finite}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$O(60)$ saddle reductions]\label{lem:toeplitz}
Row $d$ of \eqref{eq:toeplitz} contains exactly the prefix
$S_1,\dots,S_{61-d}$ together with $F$ when $d>1$, and column $c$ contains
the prefix $S_1,\dots,S_{c}$ together with $F$ when $c<60$. Hence with
prefix scans $m_j=\min_{i\le j}S_i$ and $M_j=\max_{i\le j}S_i$,
\[
\max_d\min_c M(x)_{d,c}
=\max_d
\begin{cases}
m_{60}, & d=1,\\
\min(m_{61-d},F), & d>1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\min_c\max_d M(x)_{d,c}
=\min_c
\begin{cases}
\max(M_{c},F), & c<60,\\
M_{60}, & c=60,
\end{cases}
\]
computable in $O(60)$ operations, exactly (min and max are associative and
commutative on floats), from the $61$ class values alone.
\end{lemma}

\section{The per-player dead-TTD quotient}\label{sec:quotient}

The complete game reads a player's TTD in exactly one expression: the
revival probability \eqref{eq:revival} of a failed check. That single fact
powers the entire reduction.

\begin{lemma}[Death is absorbing]\label{lem:absorbing}
If the profile $(s,t)$ is dead, then $(s',t)$ is dead for every
$s'\ge s$. Since a successful check raises a player's load and leaves their
TTD unchanged, and a failed check either ends the game for them or requires
\eqref{eq:survive}, load growth is the only motion available to a dead
profile, and it never revives it.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Quotient soundness]\label{thm:quotient}
Define $\varphi(s,t)=(s,t)$ when $(s,t)$ is alive and
$\varphi(s,t)=(s,\dead)$ otherwise, applied to each player's profile
independently. Then any two live states with equal images under $\varphi$
have identical stage matrices up to $\varphi$-identification of their
children, and hence identical values: $V$ factors through $\varphi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compare the $61$ classes of Proposition~\ref{prop:classes} at two states
that differ only in a dead player's TTD. Success classes never read TTD and
preserve both profiles' aliveness classification by
Lemma~\ref{lem:absorbing}, so they map to $\varphi$-identified children.
The failure class reads the checker's TTD only through $p$: if the checker
profile is dead, $p\equiv0$ and $F=+1$ with no child at all; if alive, the
TTD is not quotiented and $F$ is read off unchanged. Induction over the
finite acyclic graph (Corollary~\ref{cor:finite}) closes the argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Counting]\label{prop:count}
Over the transition-closed TTD domain $\{0\}\cup[60,300]$ (Lemma
\ref{lem:closure}), a player has $300\times242=72{,}600$ profiles;
$\varphi$ collapses them to $16{,}711$ alive profiles plus $300$ dead
sentinels (one per load), i.e.\ $17{,}011$ classes per player and
\[
17{,}011^2=289{,}374{,}121
\]
two-player classes --- a factor $28.2$ below the raw
$300\cdot301\cdot300\cdot301=8{,}154{,}090{,}000$ state envelope.
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Domain closure]\label{lem:closure}
Every root of interest has both TTDs in $\{0\}\cup[60,300]$, and the domain
is transition closed: revival requires $s_c+t_c\le240$, so a failure
child's TTD $t_c+s_c+60$ lies in $[60,300]$, and success preserves TTDs. A
live state with an \emph{alive} TTD in $1..59$ is thus unreachable from the
domain; the implementation rejects such states rather than approximate
them. A \emph{dead} TTD is discarded by $\varphi$ exactly, so any value is
accepted there.
\end{lemma}

All four collapse claims --- the closed form \eqref{eq:survive}, the
vanishing of \eqref{eq:revival} on dead profiles, absorption, and the
counts --- are locked by exhaustive machine enumeration over all $72{,}600$
profiles (\texttt{src/dth/tests/test\_dead\_ttd\_quotient.py},
\texttt{test\_packed\_class\_codec.py}).

\section{The potential and the sweep schedule}\label{sec:potential}

\begin{definition}[Potential]\label{def:potential}
For a profile let $\rho(s,t)=t$ if $(s,t)$ is alive and $\rho(s,t)=301$
otherwise; for a class let
\[
\Phi(x)=s_c+s_d+\rho(s_c,t_c)+\rho(s_d,t_d)\in\{0,\dots,1200\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Strict increase]\label{thm:potential}
Every live transition strictly increases $\Phi$. By cases on the moving
profile:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item successful lag $\ell$, profile stays alive or stays dead:
  $\Delta\Phi=\ell\ge1$;
\item successful lag $\ell$, alive profile dies:
  $\Delta\Phi=\ell+301-t\ge\ell+61$, since alive implies $t\le240$
  (and a dead profile never revives, so the reverse case is empty);
\item failure, revived profile alive: $\Delta\Phi=60$ (the load resets to
  zero while TTD absorbs the dose);
\item failure, revived profile dead: $\Delta\Phi=301-(s_c+t_c)\ge61$ by
  \eqref{eq:survive}.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Layered backward induction]\label{cor:layers}
No transition stays within a $\Phi$-layer, so solving whole layers in
descending $\Phi$ sees every child before any parent, and layer-$P$
residual work need only complete before layer $P-1$ begins. The
$289{,}374{,}121$ classes occupy all $1{,}201$ layers; the largest,
$\Phi=374$, holds $1{,}678{,}715$ classes. A layer is a union of bucket
rectangles: grouping profiles by their own potential $s+\rho\in[0,600]$,
layer $P$ is exactly $\bigcup_{a}\,\text{bucket}(a)\times\text{bucket}(P-a)$.
\end{corollary}

The strict increase is verified exhaustively over all $1{,}035{,}541$ live
profile transitions
(\texttt{src/dth/tests/test\_backup\_potential.py}); the schedule needs no
reachability analysis, no frontier queue, and no transposition table ---
unreachable classes are simply solved too.

\section{Certified equilibria}\label{sec:certified}

\begin{definition}[Certificate]\label{def:certificate}
For a stage matrix $M$ and mixed strategies $\sigma$ (Dropper) and $\tau$
(Checker), let
\[
L=\min_c(\sigma^{\mathsf T}M)_c,\qquad
U=\max_d(M\tau)_d,\qquad
\gap_M(\sigma,\tau)=\max(0,\,U-L).
\]
The pair \emph{certifies} the value $\widehat v=(L+U)/2$ at tolerance
$\tol$ when $\gap_M(\sigma,\tau)\le\tol$. Throughout, $\tol=10^{-6}$, and
the gap is always measured against the \emph{full} matrix
\eqref{eq:toeplitz}, never a submatrix.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Certified midpoint]\label{prop:midpoint}
$L\le\val(M)\le U$, so a certificate at tolerance $\tol$ pins
$|\widehat v-\val(M)|\le\tol/2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\sigma$ guarantees the row player at least $L$ against every reply, and
$\tau$ caps the row player at $U$; $\val(M)$ sits between the two security
levels.
\end{proof}

Two classical stability facts transfer certificate quality across
perturbed inputs; both are retained from the project's earlier whitepaper
and cited elsewhere in the repository by these names.

\begin{lemma}[Matrix-value Lipschitz bound]\label{lem:lipschitz}
If $\|M-\widehat M\|_\infty\le\epsilon$ entrywise, then
$|\val(M)-\val(\widehat M)|\le\epsilon$, since
$|\sigma^{\mathsf T}(M-\widehat M)\tau|\le\epsilon$ for all mixed pairs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Approximate-matrix saddle gap]\label{prop:twoeps}
If $(\widehat\sigma,\widehat\tau)$ is an exact equilibrium of
$\widehat M$ and $\|M-\widehat M\|_\infty\le\epsilon$, then
$\gap_M(\widehat\sigma,\widehat\tau)\le2\epsilon$: play computed from an
$\epsilon$-accurate matrix is $2\epsilon$-unexploitable in the true one.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{The solver ladder}

Each class is routed through a fixed ladder of certified solvers; the
certificate, not the solver, is the acceptance test, so a rung may fail
closed for any reason without affecting soundness.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Pure saddle.} Lemma~\ref{lem:toeplitz} computes the maximin
  and minimax of pure strategies in $O(60)$; if they meet within $\tol$
  the midpoint is certified by the corresponding pure pair.
\item \textbf{Equalizer supports.} For a guessed support pair
  $(R,C)$ with $k=\min(|R|,|C|)$, solve the two $(k{+}1)\times(k{+}1)$
  equalizer systems
  \[
  \begin{pmatrix}M_{R,C}&-\mathbf 1\\ \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}\tau_C\\ w\end{pmatrix}
  =\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf 0\\ 1\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \begin{pmatrix}M_{R,C}^{\mathsf T}&-\mathbf 1\\
  \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}\sigma_R\\ v\end{pmatrix}
  =\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf 0\\ 1\end{pmatrix},
  \]
  reject on singularity or negative mass, and certify the lifted pair
  against the full matrix in $O(60k)$ via \eqref{eq:toeplitz}. Guessed
  supports come from spatial neighbours one potential step up (the
  lag-one shift of either profile), recorded when the neighbour was
  solved. The case $k=60$ is the classical full-support structured solve
  and needs no guess.
\item \textbf{Linear programming.} The standard primal LP for the row
  player with the column strategy extracted from its inequality duals,
  falling back to two independent LPs, and finally to the same two LPs
  under tightened solver feasibility tolerances ($10^{-10}$, dual
  simplex). The retry tightens the \emph{solver}, never the gate: every
  answer is certified at $\tol$ against the full matrix, and a matrix
  failing all rungs aborts the computation rather than store an
  uncertified value.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Propagated error}

The table stores certified midpoints, so a parent's matrix is assembled
from child values that are themselves within a bounded distance of truth.

\begin{proposition}[A-priori accumulated bound]\label{prop:accumulated}
Let every stored value carry a certificate at tolerance $\tol$ and let
$\delta(x)$ bound the longest live path from $x$ to a terminal. Then the
stored value $\widehat V(x)$ satisfies
\[
|\widehat V(x)-V(x)|\;\le\;\delta(x)\,\frac{\tol}{2}
\;\le\;\frac{1200-\Phi(x)}{2}\,\tol .
\]
In particular the initial state obeys
$|\widehat V-V|(0,0,0,0)\le6.0\times10^{-4}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Induction up the DAG. Terminal entries are exact. If every child satisfies
the bound with $\delta(x)-1$, the assembled matrix is entrywise within
$(\delta(x)-1)\tol/2$ of the true one (expectations and negation are
non-expansive), Lemma~\ref{lem:lipschitz} transfers that to the value, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:midpoint} adds the state's own $\tol/2$. Each live
transition raises $\Phi$ by at least one (Theorem~\ref{thm:potential}),
bounding $\delta$.
\end{proof}

This a-priori bound is loose by design; Section~\ref{sec:computation}
reports the measured gaps, which are typically at the $10^{-12}$ scale and
never exceeded $4.9\times10^{-7}$ on any audited sample, and every stored
value's certificate is re-derivable on demand from the stored children
(Bellman recertification), so no per-class certificate needs to be
persisted.

\section{The computation}\label{sec:computation}

\subsection{Artifact and schedule}

Classes are addressed as $p_c\cdot17{,}011+p_d$ over the normative profile
enumeration (TTD-ascending, load-ascending, dead sentinels by load); the
artifact is one dense $2.16$\,GiB float64 array plus one routing byte per
class, memory mapped, with per-layer atomic checkpoints and byte-for-byte
resume. All revival probabilities \eqref{eq:revival} are evaluated once, in
one place, and every downstream kernel only gathers from those tables ---
the kernels perform no transcendental arithmetic.

\subsection{Cross-implementation audit}

The sweep is implemented twice: a Python reference that defines the
semantics, and a parallel Rust kernel constrained to \emph{bit-identical}
arithmetic --- pinned elimination order, first-maximum pivoting, no fused
multiply-add, sequential ascending reductions, and per-state warm-start
guesses precomputed so that no kernel result depends on thread count or
work partition. On every region computed by both (all synthetic
end-to-end sweeps, and a forked continuation of the production artifact)
the two implementations produced byte-identical value and routing arrays;
the contract and its test suite are
\texttt{src/dth/docs/DTH\_BACKUP\_PARITY.md}. The LP residue rung is shared
Python code by construction. This does not make the two implementations
independent in the cryptographic sense, but it does mean every accepted
value passed the certificate check in two codebases in two languages, and
that the parallel kernel cannot have introduced scheduling nondeterminism.

\begin{theorem}[Main result]\label{thm:main}
The complete Drop-the-Handkerchief game of Section~\ref{sec:game},
restricted to the transition-closed quotient domain of
Lemma~\ref{lem:closure}, is solved: every one of the $289{,}374{,}121$
classes carries a stored value certified at saddle-gap tolerance
$10^{-6}$, with the a-priori accumulated bound of
Proposition~\ref{prop:accumulated} and the measured audits above. At the
initial state,
\[
\widehat V(0,0,0,0)=0.0898500728095116,
\]
so under optimal play the first Dropper wins with probability
$\tfrac{1+\widehat V}{2}\approx0.5449$, within the stated error bounds.
Equilibrium strategies at any class are reconstructible on demand from the
stored child values through the same certified ladder.
\end{theorem}

\section{Scope of the claims}\label{sec:claims}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Rules-relative.} The theorems are about the game defined in
  Section~\ref{sec:game}, i.e.\ formulation rung L1 with the frozen
  revival surface \eqref{eq:revival}. Nothing here solves the leap-aware
  ``Surpassing the Leader'' game (rung L2 and above), which adds an
  asymmetric 61st second and is a different game.
\item \textbf{Certified numerics, not rational arithmetic.} Values are
  IEEE double midpoints with explicit saddle-gap certificates and the
  accumulated bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:accumulated}; this is a
  certified-numerics result, not an exact rational or interval-arithmetic
  proof. The structural theorems (quotient, potential, finiteness,
  Toeplitz form) are exact and machine-checked exhaustively.
\item \textbf{Domain.} The table covers the transition-closed quotient
  domain; live states with an alive TTD in $1..59$ are outside it and are
  rejected, not approximated (Lemma~\ref{lem:closure}).
\item \textbf{Values, not tabulated policies.} The artifact stores values
  and routing only; policies are re-derived (and re-certified) per query.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Reproducibility}

From the repository head: \texttt{uv run python -m dth backup} builds the
artifact (checkpointed, resumable; the configuration is
\texttt{src/dth/config/backup\_full\_v1.yaml}); the exhaustive structural
tests and both-backend parity suite run under
\texttt{uv run python -m pytest src/dth/tests -q} and
\texttt{cargo test --workspace}. The artifact manifest embeds SHA-256
digests of every array and of the solver sources that produced it.
